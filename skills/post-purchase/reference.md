# Evidence: post-purchase

Peer-reviewed findings behind the `post-purchase` procedure. Effects are as reported in the studies, in the context they were measured.

## Send abandoned-cart reminders after 24–72 hours, never within the first hour

**Rule.** No reminder in the first hour. Send between 24 and 72 hours after abandonment.
**Effect.** Reminders within an hour made people less likely to complete the purchase than sending nothing. Reminders at 24–72 h increased returns and purchases.
**Why.** Early retargeting annoys people who were already planning to come back. The memory of starting the purchase fades over several days and is gone within about a week; a reminder inside that window works while the motive is still remembered.
**Source.** Li, J., Luo, X., Lu, X., & Moriguchi, T. (2020). The double-edged effects of e-commerce cart retargeting: Does retargeting too early backfire? *Journal of Marketing*. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022242920959043

## Put a handwritten thank-you note in the box, with no promotion attached

**Rule.** Include a handwritten note (original or photocopy; personalization optional). Do not combine it with a discount or promotion.
**Effect.** Field experiment: handwritten notes increased subsequent purchasing, especially among already-loyal customers. Attaching a promotion removed the benefit.
**Why.** A handwritten note adds warmth and sincerity to a contactless transaction. A promotion alongside it makes people question the sincerity.
**Source.** Kim, S., Choi, J., & Kim, S. H. (2022). Do handwritten notes benefit online retailers? A field experiment. *Journal of Interactive Marketing*. https://doi.org/10.1177/10949968221102306

## Scent the product or packaging

**Rule.** Add a scent to packaging or product so it is remembered longer.
**Effect.** Scented products were remembered for longer, raising the odds of recommendation and repurchase.
**Why.** Smell is strongly tied to memory.
**Source.** Krishna, A., Lwin, M. O., & Morrin, M. (2010). Product scent and memory. *Journal of Consumer Research*. https://doi.org/10.1086/649909

## Offer a paid free-shipping membership with a minimum order

**Rule.** Sell a subscription that grants free delivery; require a minimum order value to qualify.
**Effect.** Members of a membership-based free-shipping program spent more and bought a wider variety; in a separate report with no primary citation on file, subscription members spent over 3× as much on the company's products — treat the 3× figure as a practitioner tip.
**Why.** The fee is a sunk cost, so members use the benefit as much as possible. Without a minimum threshold, they split orders into many small ones.
**Source.** Guo, F., & Liu, Y. (2023). The effectiveness of membership-based free shipping: An empirical investigation on consumers' purchase behaviors and revenue contribution. *Journal of Marketing*. https://doi.org/10.1177/00222429231158116

## Free, no-questions-asked returns are the expected standard

**Rule.** Offer free returns with a simple process: label included or printable, same packaging accepted, drop-off points available.
**Effect.** After an online retailer introduced free returns, customers spent more per order and bought a wider variety of products, outweighing the short-term cost.
**Why.** Knowing they can return easily, people buy despite uncertainty about fit or keeping the item; a generous policy also signals the brand's confidence in quality.
**Source.** Patel, P. C., Baldauf, C., Karlsson, S., & Oghazi, P. (2021). The impact of free returns on online purchase behavior: Evidence from an intervention at an online retailer. *Journal of Operations Management*. https://doi.org/10.1002/joom.1135

## Customers benchmark you against Amazon

**Rule.** Returns, exchanges, and refunds are where non-Amazon stores most often disappoint; make those as easy and fast as possible.
**Effect.** Survey evidence: shoppers judge all online stores against Amazon and cite returns, exchanges, and refunds as the main areas of dissatisfaction elsewhere.
**Why.** The dominant retailer sets the reference point.
**Source.** No primary citation on file; treat as a practitioner tip.

## Refund the moment the return is shipped

**Rule.** Issue the refund when the carrier scans the return, not after inspection. Limit to items that don't need inspection if abuse is a concern.
**Effect.** Instant refunds improved satisfaction and loyalty without increasing return rates.
**Why.** The wait between sending an item back and getting money back causes anxiety; an instant, simple process is judged fairer and as a sign the company cares.
**Source.** Martínez-López, F. J., Feng, C., Li, Y., & López-López, D. (2022). Using instant refunds to improve online return experiences. *Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretconser.2022.103067

## Auto-enroll everyone in a single-tier threshold loyalty program

**Rule.** Register every customer automatically; use one simple rule such as "buy 10, get 1 free"; no tiers required.
**Effect.** A non-tiered program increased customer value by 29.5% over five years. The effect comes through retention and may not show in short- or medium-term metrics.
**Why.** Members feel more connected to the company and like it for the reward; a threshold reward taps the desire for completion (someone with six purchases wants the remaining four).
**Source.** Gopalakrishnan, A., Jiang, Z., Nevskaya, Y., & Thomadsen, R. (2021). Can non-tiered customer loyalty programs be profitable? *Marketing Science*. https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2020.1268

## Make rewards variable

**Rule.** Offer an uncertain reward ("your 11th purchase is 20% or 80% off") rather than a fixed one.
**Effect.** Fewer people sign up initially, but those who do repeat the rewarded action often enough to more than offset the drop.
**Why.** Uncertainty makes the reward more engaging and the pursuit more motivating.
**Source.** Shen, L., Fishbach, A., & Hsee, C. K. (2015). The motivating-uncertainty effect: Uncertainty increases resource investment in the process of reward pursuit. *Journal of Consumer Research*. https://doi.org/10.1086/679418
