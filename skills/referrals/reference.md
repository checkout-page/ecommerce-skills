# Evidence: referrals

Peer-reviewed findings behind the `referrals` procedure. Effects are as reported in the studies, in the context they were measured.

## Frame the referral around the friend's benefit

**Rule.** Headline the incentive for the friend ("give a friend $20 off"); keep the referrer's reward but don't make it the message.
**Effect.** In two field experiments, an altruistic framing made people up to 60% more likely to make at least one referral, and total referrals rose 86%.
**Why.** Recommending a product is an altruistic act. A reward for the referrer clashes with that motive and creates guilt; leading with the friend's benefit removes the conflict.
**Source.** Jung, J., Bapna, R., Golden, J. M., & Sun, T. (2020). Words matter! Toward a prosocial call-to-action for online referral: Evidence from two field experiments. *Information Systems Research*. https://doi.org/10.1287/isre.2019.0873

## Pre-fill the share message with one signal

**Rule.** Include either "the referrer already bought this" or "you can earn a reward for referring others" — not both.
**Effect.** Stating that the referrer had bought the product raised the friend's conversion likelihood by 11%. Stating that the friend could earn a referral reward increased further referrals by 53%. Combining them did not add up.
**Why.** Each signal serves a different goal (conversion vs. chain referrals); together they dilute the message and reintroduce the self-interest conflict.
**Source.** Same research program as above (Jung, Bapna, Golden, & Sun, 2020). https://doi.org/10.1287/isre.2019.0873

## Prefer non-monetary rewards

**Rule.** Offer a free gift, upgrade, or other non-cash promotion rather than money or a cash discount.
**Effect.** Non-monetary rewards performed better than cash for referrals.
**Why.** Cash makes the referral feel like a transaction; a gift keeps it feeling like a favor.
**Source.** No primary citation on file; treat as a practitioner tip.

## Very high rewards attract unprofitable customers

**Rule.** Keep the reward modest; more referrals at a high reward are not worth it if the referred customers are worse.
**Effect.** When an online bank raised its referral reward from €20 to €50, the referral rate rose but the average profitability of referred customers fell 48%, making acquisition unprofitable.
**Why.** Large rewards motivate referrers to recruit anyone, including people who wouldn't otherwise be good customers.
**Source.** Study on referral reward size (2020). Referral reward size and new customer profitability. *Marketing Science*. https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2020.1242
