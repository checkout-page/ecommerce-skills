# Evidence: store-audit

Peer-reviewed findings behind the `store-audit` procedure. Effects are as reported in the studies, in the context they were measured.

## Channel priorities depend on hedonic vs utilitarian purchases

**Rule.** Hedonic products: social media and your own product pages are the crucial channels. Utilitarian products: search engines, comparison sites, third-party reviews, and deal sites.
**Effect.** Online customer journeys differed systematically by purchase type; the channels above carried disproportionate weight for each.
**Why.** Hedonic buying is impulsive and emotional, seeking an enjoyable experience; it starts on social and is won by simple signals and appealing design. Utilitarian buying is rational and often planned; buyers want easy comparisons to maximize value.
**Source.** Li, J., Abbasi, A., Cheema, A., & Abraham, L. B. (2020). Path to purpose? How online customer journeys differ for hedonic versus utilitarian purchases. *Journal of Marketing*. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022242920911628

## Label recommendations as algorithmic for utilitarian, human for hedonic

**Rule.** "Recommended based on our data" for functional purchases; "Our expert's pick" for enjoyment purchases.
**Effect.** Preference for the recommendation source flipped with the purchase goal (e.g. a business hotel stay vs a holiday stay).
**Why.** People believe machines are better at weighing functional pros and cons but don't understand what people enjoy.
**Source.** Longoni, C., & Cian, L. (2020). Artificial intelligence in utilitarian vs. hedonic contexts: The "word-of-machine" effect. *Journal of Marketing*. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022242920957347

## Horizontal layouts favor quality; vertical layouts favor price

**Rule.** Use a horizontal display (carousel) when you want people to weigh quality and the primary feature; vertical (scroll) when you want price or secondary features to dominate.
**Effect.** With a horizontal layout, people were up to 26% more likely to choose the highest-quality, more expensive laptop.
**Why.** Horizontal presentation primes abstract, high-level thinking (benefits, quality); vertical primes concrete, detail-oriented thinking (how to buy, price).
**Source.** Mukherjee, S. (2022). Vertical vs horizontal presentation formats result in different consumption choices due to construal priming. *Journal of Consumer Behaviour*. https://doi.org/10.1002/cb.2062

## Match structured or unstructured design to brand positioning

**Rule.** Structured design (geometric shapes, straight lines, symmetry) for a reliable, effective positioning; unstructured (rounded, incomplete shapes, unpredictable layout) for a fun, exciting one.
**Effect.** Perfume ads received 39% more clicks when the design matched the positioning.
**Why.** Visual structure is read as a cue to the brand's character.
**Source.** No primary citation on file; treat as a practitioner tip.

## Show your costs

**Rule.** Display a per-product cost breakdown by category; group categories once there are more than ~8. Holds for margins up to 55%.
**Effect.** Sales of a $115 wallet were 22% higher with a cost infographic beside it.
**Why.** Disclosure of costs increases trust and liking.
**Source.** Mohan, B., Buell, R. W., & John, L. K. (2020). Lifting the veil: The benefits of cost transparency. *Marketing Science*. https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2019.1200

## Say you're small — unless the product is high-tech

**Rule.** Small company, low-tech product: communicate the size. Small company, high-tech product: emphasize R&D spend. Large company, low-tech product: show employee passion.
**Effect.** Consumers inferred higher quality from small companies for low-tech products and from large companies for high-tech products.
**Why.** Employee motivation is assumed higher at small firms; R&D resources are assumed higher at large firms.
**Source.** Woolley, K., Kupor, D., & Liu, P. J. (2022). Does company size shape product quality inferences? Larger companies make better high-tech products, but smaller companies make better low-tech products. *Journal of Marketing Research*. https://doi.org/10.1177/00222437221124857

## Offer an extraordinary warranty

**Rule.** A warranty far longer than the category norm signals quality; best for buyers who know the category but not the brand.
**Effect.** People were 34.8% more likely to buy a hoodie with a 10-year warranty.
**Why.** A long warranty is a costly, credible signal of product strength.
**Source.** Study on extraordinary warranties as a quality signal for weakly familiar brands (2022). Overcoming the challenge of low familiarity: Can a weakly familiar brand signal quality with exceptionally strong warranty? *Journal of Business Research*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2021.11.078

## State real sustainability practices

**Rule.** If a claim is true (carbon-neutral, recyclable packaging, organic), put it on the product and packaging.
**Effect.** Across 10 million grocery sales, products with sustainability claims had 6.4% higher revenue. People enjoyed a sustainable pen 28% more and a song 12% more on eco headphones when told. In one study 82% more people signed up for a small business's newsletter when it had sustainable practices.
**Why.** Sustainability is read as a quality and trust signal, especially for small firms, and improves the experience of using the product.
**Source.** No primary citation on file; treat as a practitioner tip.

## Use marketplaces to acquire direct customers

**Rule.** New or unknown brands should list on a major marketplace and make switching to direct purchase attractive.
**Effect.** On average 1 in 71 marketplace sales generated a new direct customer. Acquisition cost on marketplaces averaged 24% of revenue including commissions, versus 30% for mature direct brands and up to 100% for new ones.
**Why.** Marketplaces provide cheap discovery; some customers will move to the brand's own channel if friction is low and there's a reason.
**Source.** Maier, E., & Wieringa, J. (2021). Acquiring customers through online marketplaces? The effect of marketplace sales on sales in a retailer's own channels. *International Journal of Research in Marketing*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijresmar.2020.09.007
