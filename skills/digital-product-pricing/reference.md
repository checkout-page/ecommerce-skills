# Evidence: digital-product-pricing

Delta only. Shared digit, gift, "$0", deadline, and depth findings are not repeated here.

## Unknown sellers need the quality-ending

**Rule.** An individual or unknown publisher should default to a rounded price; just-below is for promotions or after the buyer has already used the product.
**Effect.** Rounded prices "feel right" for quality- and feeling-driven purchases; a 99-ending is read as a low-price appeal.
**Why.** Digital goods have no materials or shipping cost to read as quality, so the ending does more of that work.
**Source.** See `pricing` — Wadhwa & Zhang (2015), https://doi.org/10.1086/678484 · Schindler (2006), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretai.2005.11.001

## After a sample, just-below is fine

**Rule.** Freemium upgrades and post-sample offers can use a just-below ending; the left digit must actually drop.
**Effect / Why / Source.** Left-digit and 99-ending findings in `pricing`.

## Distant pre-orders: gift not discount; lead magnets "$0"; pick-your-price not PWYW

**Rule.** Same conditions as `promotions`: gift when launch is 3+ weeks out; "for $0" not "free"; 3–4 fixed options, never open pay-what-you-want.
**Source.** See `promotions` (Mukherjee et al. 2021; Koo & Suk 2020; Wang, Beck & Yuan 2021).

## Discount depth and urgency

**Rule.** ≤ 40%; deadline with a reason, never a seat cap. Quality is hard to judge before buying a file, so the depth warning is especially relevant.
**Source.** See `discounts`.
