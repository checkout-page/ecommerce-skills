# Product visuals

Produce a photo/video brief in which each shot is tied to a finding — most with a measured effect, a few marked † as practitioner tips. Format, gaze, the viewer's sense of touching the product, and composition (shine, spacing, side, props) move sales and willingness to pay more than lighting taste does.

## When to use

- Writing a shot list or creative brief; choosing video vs stills or between candidate images
- Reviewing why a product image or ad creative underperforms

## When not to use

- Page layout, CTAs, variants, price position → `product-page`
- Description text → `product-copy`

## Inputs

Collect, or state your assumption:

- The product; can it be picked up and held? Is it premium?
- One main function (energy drink) or many?
- Worn, and by whom (womenswear vs menswear)?
- Where the visuals run (product page, ads, social); audience reading direction

> **Classify the product first.** *Hedonic* = bought for enjoyment, pleasure, or the experience (chocolate, fashion, decor, travel, spa, perfume). *Utilitarian* = bought to do a job, judged on function and value (detergent, appliances, insect repellent, tools, accounting software). If it's mixed, decide by what the buyer is optimizing for at the moment of purchase.

## Procedure

1. **Format.** Hedonic → video, GIF, or slideshow is required (willingness to pay 79% higher). Utilitarian → stills are enough. Premium hedonic → add a slow-motion action shot (+22% sales) †. Video should feel personal and human, not corporate. †
2. **Gaze.** Hedonic → model looks away from the camera (sun hat: +30% sales). Informational or negative-emotion messages → model looks at the camera.
3. **Touch.** A first-person hand touching, holding, or using the product (+16% purchase likelihood).
4. **Side.** Graspable product toward the right of the frame, angled for a right hand. If it can't be held, add a graspable object beside it (handedness doesn't change with reading direction — don't flip this). Future-oriented products right, vintage left; flip *that* for right-to-left readers.
5. **Shine.** Light or polish for a visible reflection; a digital reflection is fine only if it stays representative of the real finish (a matte product shown glossy misleads buyers). Never brief "no reflections" (+23% attractiveness, +32% purchase likelihood).
6. **Spacing.** Clear empty space between products in multi-product shots and grids (liking +11.4%, sales nearly doubled). In ads, wide margins with related products clustered in the center. †
7. **Multiples.** Product bought for one specific function (an energy drink for energy, a stain remover) → several copies together (+10.7% perceived effectiveness) †. Not a rule for treats or decor.
8. **Props.** Complementary items around the focal product, varied in shape and color; the props' own sales are unaffected.
9. **Context.** Womenswear → a fitting setting, not white background. Menswear → irrelevant.
10. **Saturation.** High to look bigger, low to look smaller. Pretest candidates with a poll where cheap. †

## Output

| # | Shot | Direction | Rule | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero video, 8–12 s | Truffle broken in slow motion | Hedonic → video; premium → slow-mo | WTP +79% |
| 2 | Hand shot | First-person hand lifting a truffle, box on the right | Touch; graspable right | +16% |

Then list what *not* to do for this product (e.g. no direct gaze, no matte finish).

## Common mistakes

- Making video "secondary" for a hedonic product — it is the highest-effect item.
- Briefing "avoid reflections" — shine raises attractiveness and purchase intent.
- Centering a graspable product — it belongs toward the right.
- A single can for a single-function product — show several.

## Quick reference

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Format | Hedonic → video; utilitarian → stills |
| Premium hedonic | Slow-motion action shot |
| Gaze | Away for hedonic; at camera for info or negative emotion |
| Hand | First-person hand touching |
| Position | Graspable product right (never flipped); future-right/past-left flips for RTL |
| Finish | Visible shine |
| Layout | Space apart; wide ad margins |
| Single-function | Multiple copies |
| Props | Complementary, varied |
| Womenswear | Contextual background |
| Size cue | High saturation = bigger |

† No primary citation on file for this rule — a practitioner tip, not a measured finding.
