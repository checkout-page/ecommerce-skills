# Evidence: event-pricing

Delta only. Shared digit, gift, deadline, sold-out, and recommendation findings are not repeated in full.

## Rounded prices for experiences

**Rule.** Tickets default to rounded; just-below is a promotion ending.
**Source.** See `pricing` — Wadhwa & Zhang (2015), https://doi.org/10.1086/678484 · Schindler (2006), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretai.2005.11.001

## Sold-out dates at 10–30%

**Rule.** Show sold-out sessions marked as sold out; keep the share between 10% and 30%.
**Effect.** Sales up to 31% higher among shoppers who hadn't settled on a specific option; the effect reverses when too many options are gone.
**Why.** A sold-out date is a quality signal; too many removes the feeling of choice.
**Source.** See `product-page` — Tian, Chen & Xu (2022), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijresmar.2021.04.002

## Seat-count promos vs accurate inventory

**Rule.** Do not use "only N seats" as a promotional scarcity claim. Accurate remaining capacity in a seat picker is inventory, not that claim.
**Effect.** People who missed a quantity-limited *offer* reported anger and switching intentions; time limits did not.
**Source.** See `discounts` — Biraglia, Usrey & Ulqinaku (2021), https://doi.org/10.1002/mar.21489

## Gift not early-bird when the date is 3+ weeks out

**Rule.** Same 3-week gift-vs-discount rule as other pre-orders, applied to early-bird ticketing.
**Source.** See `promotions` — Mukherjee, Smith & Burton (2021), https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-021-00810-1

## Human labels for experiences

**Rule.** "Others also booked" / "instructor's pick" for hedonic events.
**Source.** See `store-audit` — Longoni & Cian (2020), https://doi.org/10.1177/0022242920957347
